Method / New Forms Research

Forms that could not
exist before AI.

This is not a pile of "I tried AI" writeups. Each note identifies a form that was impossible to finish before, builds it into something you can actually open and play, and then hands over the method.

Finishing is the fastest way to learn.
An unfinished experiment returns no primary data. A prototype nobody plays cannot tell you where people left or where they cried. So everything here is finished and public.

Notes in English

Each one follows the same shape: constraint → what got removed → the new form → evidence → how to repeat it.

A-1
Stories that watch you / flags made of words

A story can finally remember you

Branching died from word count, not from technology. When the cost of prose collapsed, the ceiling on flags disappeared — and metafiction stopped being a talent and became a feature you implement.
A-8
Auditing the links / counting the space between things

Nobody can reach your other work

16 projects, 90 videos, and almost no lines drawn between them. 3 of 12 pages were dead ends; 7 of 14 short/long pairs were never joined. Link counts are integers, so make them a test.

How each note is written

  1. What was impossible before AI (the constraint)
  2. Which constraint AI actually removed (be precise — it is usually one)
  3. What that makes possible (the new form)
  4. The thing I built (evidence — you can open it)
  5. Steps, timings, failures, and code you can paste

The work itself

Free, no account, runs in a browser.

A Hundred Sins A ~30 minute sound novel. Seven languages, fully voiced in English and Japanese. Music Universe 19,810 artists on one map. Press the dice and something you have never heard starts playing. Cabin in the Hollow A 360° room you can look around. Firelight synced to a breathing guide. Outside the Answer 15 episodes, 1,593 voice lines. Someone starts writing in the margins.

More notes exist in Japanese — ten of them, covering music mapping, one-day novel pipelines, mining demand from tens of thousands of titles, quality gates for AI output, and designing time spent alone. They are being translated as I go: read the Japanese index.
